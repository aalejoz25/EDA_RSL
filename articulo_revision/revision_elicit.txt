Advanced AI models, primarily transfer learning-based CNNs, effectively overcome traditional visual detection limitations for binary disease classification with accuracies of 90-99%, but multimodal data fusion remains underutilized
and the critical objectives of quantifying disease severity progression and predicting impact on internal quality and
yield are largely unaddressed, with fewer than 15% of studies implementing severity staging and only three examining correlations with internal quality parameters.

Abstract
Advanced AI models, predominantly convolutional neural networks employing transfer learning with architectures
such as VGG, ResNet, and YOLO variants, demonstrate substantial improvements over traditional visual inspection
methods for disease detection in horticultural crops, with reported accuracies ranging from 90-99% under controlled
conditions. These approaches effectively address limitations of manual assessment including inconsistency, labor
intensity, and delayed detection. However, the transition from binary disease classification to quantitative severity
assessment remains underdeveloped, with only 12 of 80 reviewed studies implementing multi-level severity classification, and the correlation between detected disease severity and internal quality or yield impact was examined
in only three studies. Hyperspectral imaging combined with machine learning shows strong potential for early detection and nutritional parameter prediction, achieving R²=0.7 for physiological indicators and detecting pathogen
stress before visible symptoms appear, yet hardware costs, miniaturization challenges, and environmental sensitivity
limit field implementation.
True multimodal data fusion integrating RGB imaging, hyperspectral data, and IoT sensors was implemented in
only eight studies, representing a critical gap between theoretical potential and practical application. Significant
methodological limitations persist across the literature: dataset sizes frequently fall below 3,000 images, external
validation on independent datasets appears in only eight studies, code availability is reported in fewer than 10%
of publications, and models trained under controlled laboratory conditions show performance degradation when

1

deployed in field environments with variable lighting and complex backgrounds. Emerging best practices include
k-fold cross-validation, expert-supervised ground truth establishment, and lightweight architectures optimized for
edge deployment, though standardized evaluation protocols remain absent. The evidence indicates that while AI
substantially advances disease detection capabilities, the critical objectives of quantifying disease impact on internal
quality and predicting yield losses remain largely unaddressed by current research.

Emerging architectures demonstrated notable performance improvements. The Vision Transformer (ViT) achieved
100% classification accuracy on the MangoLeafBD dataset, attributed to its parameter efficiency compared to deep
CNN models. Attention mechanisms integrated with DenseNet architectures yielded 98.7% accuracy for potato
disease detection, while the IMNM model combining improved ResNet with dynamic convolution networks achieved
98.55% accuracy with cross-species generalization to 99.81%.

Hybrid and Ensemble Approaches
Feature fusion strategies demonstrated promising results. The FFIR model combining Inception-ResNet with canonical correlation analysis achieved 99.34% accuracy for strawberry disease detection. Hybrid CNN-SVM architectures
achieved 95.93% accuracy for mango scab severity classification, while CNN-Random Forest combinations yielded
86.97% accuracy for banana ripeness detection and 77.2% for pear disease recognition.

Synthesis of Heterogeneous Findings
Explaining Performance Variability
The wide range of reported accuracies (77.2%-100%) reflects systematic methodological differences rather than contradictory findings:
Dataset Complexity and Size Effects: Studies achieving perfect or near-perfect accuracy (100%) typically employed
smaller, homogeneous datasets under controlled conditions. The ViT model's 100% accuracy on MangoLeafBD was
achieved on a well-balanced dataset of 4,000 images with 500 samples per class. In contrast, studies addressing
severity classification with symptom similarity achieved lower performance—the EfficientDet D1 model's 66.03%
mAP for Begomovirus severity reflected the inherent challenge of distinguishing similar symptom presentations.
Architecture-Task Matching: Higher performance correlated with appropriate architecture selection for specific
tasks:
• Object detection tasks (fruit localization, harvesting support) achieved optimal results with YOLO-family models
• Multi-class classification benefited from transfer learning with VGG/ResNet architectures
• Severity quantification requiring fine-grained discrimination showed improved results with attention mechanisms and hybrid approaches
Environmental Conditions: Field-collected datasets introduced performance variability. The AppleNet model
trained on real-world orchard images with background noise achieved 96.99% accuracy, while models trained on
controlled-condition datasets reported higher accuracies that may not generalize to field settings.

Contextual Validity of Findings
The apparent contradiction between studies reporting AI superiority over traditional methods and those acknowledging persistent limitations reflects different operational contexts:
Early-stage vs. Established Disease Detection: AI approaches demonstrated clear advantages for established disease
symptoms with distinctive visual patterns. However, early-stage detection before visible symptoms requires spectral
imaging capabilities, where hyperspectral approaches showed promise but faced practical implementation barriers.
Controlled vs. Field Environments: Laboratory-tested models achieved higher reported accuracies but faced degradation in field conditions due to lighting variability, background complexity, and environmental factors. The Martin

18

Valtierra-Rodriguez et al. (2025) study explicitly acknowledged that results obtained under controlled conditions
”may not perform well in real field scenarios”.
Single Disease vs. Multi-Disease Classification: Models targeting single disease types consistently outperformed
multi-disease classifiers. The EfficientNetB0 model achieved 99.88% accuracy for citrus canker and HLB, with the
authors noting ”high accuracy obtained is mainly because only two diseases were considered”.

Emerging Standards and Best Practices
Several methodological standards emerged across higher-quality studies:
• Cross-validation requirements: Studies employing k-fold cross-validation or external validation provided
more reliable performance estimates
• Data augmentation protocols: Systematic augmentation addressing class imbalance improved model robustness
• Expert ground truth: Studies with plant pathologist supervision or controlled inoculation provided more
reliable training labels
• Lightweight model development: Architectures optimized for edge deployment enabled practical field implementation

Critical Gaps Requiring Resolution
Multimodal Fusion: Despite theoretical advantages, true multimodal fusion combining imaging modalities with
IoT sensor data remained underdeveloped. Only eight studies implemented substantive data integration, with most
employing simple concatenation rather than learned fusion strategies.
Internal Quality Prediction: The correlation between external disease detection and internal quality parameters was
rarely examined. Only three studies addressed internal quality prediction, representing a significant gap between
disease detection capabilities and yield/quality impact assessment.
Severity Quantification: Progression from binary classification to quantitative severity assessment remained limited,
with only 12 studies implementing multi-level severity classification. The relationship between detected severity and
subsequent yield impact was not established in any reviewed study.
Generalizability Validation: Cross-species, cross-region, and cross-season validation was reported in only four studies, limiting confidence in model transferability. The IMNM model's cross-species validation achieving 99.81% average accuracy for apple, wheat, and rice represented an exception requiring broader replication.
